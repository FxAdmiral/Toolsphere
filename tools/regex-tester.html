About regular expressions

A regular expression (regex) is a short pattern that describes text. Developers use them to search, validate and clean up text: finding email addresses in a file, checking that a phone number has the right shape, or pulling values out of a log line.

A few basics: . matches any character, \d a digit, \w a letter, digit or underscore, + means one or more, * zero or more, ^ and $ the start and end, and brackets ( ) capture a group.

This tester uses JavaScript's regex rules, which are close to but not identical to other languages. It runs in a separate background task and stops after 2 seconds, so a pattern that is accidentally very slow will not freeze the page.
